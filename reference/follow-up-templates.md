# Follow-up Templates

Templates for the post-call workflow. Variables in `[brackets]` get filled in by the specialist when generating output, or by you when adapting outside this folder.

All templates are written in the indie AI consultant voice — direct, specific, no consultant-speak, no "circling back" or "touching base." If you keep one rule, keep this: every follow-up references at least one verbatim phrase from the call. Generic follow-ups feel generic.

---

## 1. 24-hour post-call email — English

> Subject: [Company name] × [problem area] — next step
>
> [First name],
>
> Quick recap: [restate their core pain in their words, one sentence]. You want [their stated outcome] by [their stated deadline].
>
> Phase 1 I'd open at $[range USD]: [2-3 line concrete scope description, ending with the deliverable]. [Duration] build, [milestone] by week [N], with a written off-ramp if [their key metric or signal] isn't moving.
>
> Three call windows below, [their timezone]. Pick whichever works.
>
> — [Your name]

**Rules of use:**
- Send within 24 hours of the call. After 48 hours the urgency cools; after a week, you've taught them you're not on top of it.
- Reference one verbatim phrase. Without it, the email reads as a template.
- Email *is* the proposal. If they ask for more, send a 1-pager — never a deck.

## 3. 72-hour nudge (no response) — English

> Subject: Re: [original subject]
>
> [First name],
>
> Following up on Tuesday's note. Two of the three windows are still open; the third is gone, so this is the updated set:
>
> - [Date 1]
> - [Date 2]
> - [Date 3]
>
> If timing isn't right, no problem — let me know and I'll close the loop. If something else came up, also no problem; happy to pick this back up when you're ready.
>
> — [Your name]

**Rules of use:**
- Always offer to close the loop. Forces a yes/no instead of indefinite drift.
- Do not send a third nudge. After two emails with no response, archive and move on. They have your contact.

## 5. Scope outline document — 1-page skeleton

When the second call confirms a yes, send this within 48 hours. Aim for one page printed.

```
[Project name] — Phase 1 Scope

Problem
- [One sentence stating the buyer's core pain.]
- [One sentence on the metric/outcome they want to move.]

What I'll deliver in Phase 1
- [Deliverable 1]
- [Deliverable 2]
- [Deliverable 3]
(End with the most demoable artifact — what they show their team or board.)

What's NOT in Phase 1
- [Out-of-scope item 1]
- [Out-of-scope item 2]
(This section is more important than the IN-scope list. It prevents creep.)

Timeline
- Week 1: [milestone]
- Week 2: [milestone]
- Week N: [final delivery]

Investment
- $[USD amount], invoiced [50% upfront / 50% on delivery].

Off-ramp
- If [specific condition] by [milestone date], either party can cancel with no further obligation.

Next steps
- Sign within [N] business days, kickoff call within [N] days of signing.
```

Keep it on one page. Buyers don't read more.

---

## 6. 3-date call invitation — pattern

When proposing call dates, follow this pattern:

- Three dates, never more, never fewer. Two looks ungenerous, four looks desperate.
- Span across the week (avoid 3 dates in 2 days — looks pushy or like you're empty).
- Match their timezone and working hours from the call. If they said "10 AM ET," propose 10 AM ET.
- For LATAM cross-border: dual-stamp the timezone (e.g., "11:00 AM Mendoza / 10:00 AM ET").
- Use specific calendar dates, not "next week." Specificity reduces friction.
