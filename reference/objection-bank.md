# Objection Bank

Twelve objections that come up across LATAM and US discovery calls. For each: the surface form (how it gets said), the real fear underneath, and the response pattern that consistently moves the conversation forward.

The job is not to "overcome" objections — it's to surface the underlying fear and answer that fear directly. If the surface objection is the real concern, the response works. If it's a smokescreen for something else (no real budget, no decision-maker), the response also works because it forces the truth out.

---

## 1. "Es muy caro" / "It's too expensive"

**Real fear:** can't justify the spend to a partner, board, or themselves. Not actually about the price — about the case for the price.

**Response:** "Compared to what?" Then offer two paths: (a) smaller-scope Phase 1 that proves value within their comfort zone, (b) a payback timeline ("you spend X, you save/earn Y in month 3"). Don't drop price without scope reduction.

---

## 2. "Ya probamos GPT y no funcionó" / "We already tried ChatGPT and it didn't work"

**Real fear:** wasted money on AI hype before, looks foolish to leadership, doesn't want to repeat.

**Response:** name the gap explicitly — "raw model + no workflow ≠ a working system." Then describe what the workflow layer adds (data, retrieval, evaluation, guardrails, integrations). One concrete example from a similar client.

---

## 3. "Mi sobrino/cuñado lo hace por $200" / "My nephew can build it for $200"

**Real fear:** getting taken advantage of by an outsider when family-and-friends can "do the same thing."

**Response:** opportunity cost framing. "If your nephew builds it and it doesn't ship in 8 weeks, what does that delay cost you?" Don't disparage the nephew. Reframe the question from "price of build" to "price of delay."

---

## 4. "¿Cómo sé que va a funcionar?" / "How do I know it will work?"

**Real fear:** reputation risk. If this fails, the buyer looks bad to their team or boss.

**Response:** pilot scope with a clear off-ramp. "Phase 1 is two weeks, scoped to one workflow, with a written kill-switch if metric X isn't moving by week 6." Risk reversal, not promises.

---

## 5. "Tenemos que hablarlo internamente" / "We need to discuss internally"

**Real fear:** rarely about discussion — usually means there's a stakeholder not on the call who has veto power, OR they're polite-rejecting.

**Response:** "Of course. Who else needs to be on the next call so we don't repeat this conversation?" If they name someone → real, schedule call two with that person. If they dodge → polite rejection; let them go and ask if there's a referral.

---

## 6. "Mandanos una propuesta y la revisamos" / "Send a proposal and we'll review"

**Real fear:** wants to compare apples-to-apples with other consultors, or wants to delay the decision.

**Response:** "I send a 1-page scope after we agree on the problem. Right now I think the problem is X — agree?" Force alignment on the problem before sending anything. Long proposals get filed and forgotten.

---

## 7. "No tenemos tiempo ahora, hablemos en [3 meses]" / "Let's talk in 3 months"

**Real fear:** either real (they're truly slammed) or polite-decline (the problem isn't urgent enough).

**Response:** test which it is. "Got it. What changes in 3 months that makes this the right time?" If they answer concretely → schedule with calendar invite NOW for that date. If they hedge → it's a no, accept it cleanly and ask for a referral.

---

## 8. "¿Por qué tan caro vs [competidor]?" / "Why so expensive vs X?"

**Real fear:** wants validation of price difference, often shopping comparable scope from multiple sources.

**Response:** acknowledge the alternative is real. Then: "Different scope. Theirs probably ends at X, mine includes Y and Z. Want me to scope down to match?" Make the price difference about scope, never about quality (saying "they're worse" sounds defensive).

---

## 9. "Te pago cuando funcione" / "I'll pay when it works"

**Real fear / signal:** they don't see consultor work as work. They see it as outcome-only. This is a red flag, not an objection.

**Response:** decline the framing. "I work on time-and-materials or fixed-scope. Not contingent. If you're looking for outcome-only contracts, you'll need someone with skin in your specific outcome — that's an equity conversation, not a consulting one." Walk if they push.

---

## 10. "¿Y por qué no usamos [tool del mes]?" / "Why don't we just use [tool]?"

**Real fear:** they read about a tool and want validation that they don't need a consultor at all.

**Response:** "You can. Want me to walk you through what it'd take to set it up yourself?" Either: (a) they say yes, you give them a 15-min plan, lose the deal but earn trust and a referral, (b) they realize the tool alone doesn't solve the workflow gap and re-engage.

---

## 11. "Es complicado el tema de la facturación" / "Billing is complicated"

**Real fear (LATAM-specific):** USD invoicing friction or genuine ARS-only operation.

**Response:** offer both: "I invoice USD with wire or USDT, or ARS at official rate + 30%. Pick the one that's cleaner for your accounting." For mid-tier clients, USDT is often the path of least resistance. Don't absorb the FX cost yourself.

---

## 12. "Necesitamos garantía de resultados" / "We need a results guarantee"

**Real fear:** they've been burned, or someone is making them ask this for cover.

**Response:** offer a guarantee on *process and milestones*, never on outcomes you don't control. "I guarantee delivery of Phase 1 scope by week 6, with weekly progress reports and a no-questions-asked kill switch. I don't guarantee that AI will move your conversion rate by X% — nobody honest does, because that depends on factors outside this scope." Trust comes from saying no.
