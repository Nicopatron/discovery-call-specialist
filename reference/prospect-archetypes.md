# Prospect Archetypes

Six archetypes I see across discovery calls. Each carries its own pain layers, anchoring strategy, and what NOT to pitch. Red flags applicable across all archetypes are summarized in the appendix at the bottom.

---

## 1. Skeptical-but-frustrated SaaS founder

**Signals**
- Tech company, 5–50 people, post product-market-fit, growing.
- Mentions competitor moves verbatim ("X just launched Y").
- Has tried a consultancy or agency before, has a story about being burned.
- Decision-maker on the call, often impatient with discovery.
- Concrete deadline (board meeting, fundraise, launch).

**Pain layers**
- *Functional:* shipping AI features that match competitors.
- *Emotional:* fear of being seen as the laggard in their category.
- *Status:* needs to be the founder who "saw the AI moment coming."
- *LATAM-specific (when applicable):* less hype-pressure than US founders, but more pressure from US investors expecting the same AI narrative.

**Anchor strategy**
$4–8K Phase 1 for "decide what to build, then build a thin slice." Up to $15K if Phase 1 includes a working prototype.

**What NOT to pitch**
- Long discovery sprints. They've been burned by deck-only deliverables.
- "Strategy roadmaps." They want code in week one.
- AI-buzzword decks. Speak in features, customers, and metrics.

## 2. Overwhelmed service operator

**Signals**
- Service business: dental clinic, agency, professional studio, consulting firm.
- 5–50 patients/clients/cases per week.
- "We're losing X by Y" with concrete numbers (turns, leads, follow-ups).
- Operations rest on 1–2 people doing too much.
- No urgency, just chronic friction.

**Pain layers**
- *Functional:* coordinator/receptionist/admin is saturated and missing things.
- *Emotional:* owner doesn't feel in control of their own business.
- *Status:* internal pressure (family, partners) to "modernize."
- *LATAM-specific:* the saturated coordinator is often family or trusted long-term staff — replacing or upgrading them is sensitive.

**Anchor strategy**
$2–4K Phase 1 for one tightly scoped automation done well, not three half-done.

**What NOT to pitch**
- Replacement of staff. Pitch augmentation: "the coordinator stops doing X, gets time back for Y."
- Generic "AI for clinics" deck. Pitch the specific workflow that's bleeding.
- Multi-vendor integration projects. Keep it one tool, one workflow, one outcome.

## 3. Cross-border SMB scaler

**Signals**
- Family-owned or founder-led business in LATAM selling to US (or vice versa).
- Already exporting / has crossed the border once.
- Bilingual or English-fluent owner.
- Concrete metric they want to move (repurchase rate, conversion, AOV).
- Already invoicing in USD or willing to.

**Pain layers**
- *Functional:* the digital experience doesn't match the product quality.
- *Emotional:* embarrassment about the gap. They know it's bad.
- *Status:* the owner travels to the US, sees how peers operate, comes back motivated.
- *LATAM-specific:* asymmetric expectations — US customers expect Shopify-tier experience, LATAM teams operate Excel-tier.

**Anchor strategy**
$5–10K Phase 1 + optional monthly retainer ($600–1500). They have USD budget and willingness to pay for quality.

**What NOT to pitch**
- "Localization" framed as translation. They live the bilingualism — they want operational fluency, not subtitles.
- Generic e-commerce automation. Tie everything to *their* metric.
- Long contracts. They want to validate Phase 1 before committing to ongoing.

## 4. AI-curious legacy / family business owner

**Signals**
- Established business (10+ years), often family-run, traditional industry.
- Owner hears "AI" everywhere and wonders if they're missing out.
- Vague problem framing: "I want to do something with AI."
- No clear use case but real budget.
- Long sales cycle expected — 2–4 calls before close.

**Pain layers**
- *Functional:* unclear; need to extract during discovery.
- *Emotional:* fear of being left behind, often nudged by a peer or family member.
- *Status:* wants to be the one who "brought AI to the company."
- *LATAM-specific:* family pressure is often the real driver ("my son/nephew told me I should look at AI").

**Anchor strategy**
$3–6K for a paid discovery sprint (NOT free — paid signals seriousness). Output: a written assessment of the top 2 candidate AI use cases for their operation.

**What NOT to pitch**
- A specific solution before discovery. They'll say yes to anything and you'll regret it.
- Free discovery. They have budget; charging filters tire-kickers.
- Deep tech (RAG, vector DBs, fine-tuning). Speak in outcomes.

## 5. Hands-off marketer (CMO outsourcing AI)

**Signals**
- Marketing leader at a mid-market SMB or scale-up (not VP-level enterprise).
- Has a content / acquisition / lifecycle stack already.
- Wants AI integrated *into existing workflows*, not replacing them.
- Decision-maker on day-to-day spend up to $15K, escalates above.
- Pragmatic, less emotional than founder archetypes.

**Pain layers**
- *Functional:* output volume / personalization at current headcount is capped.
- *Emotional:* relatively low — this is a job, not a mission.
- *Status:* wants to look like the CMO who "operationalized AI" to their CEO.
- *LATAM-specific:* often manages a small in-house team plus external freelancers in Argentina/Mexico for cost efficiency.

**Anchor strategy**
$5–10K per use case, modular. They'll add use cases over time once Phase 1 proves out.

**What NOT to pitch**
- Replacing their team. They're protective of headcount.
- Strategy advice. They have a strategy. They want execution.
- Ambiguous scoping. They'll need a clean SOW for procurement.

---

## 6. Tire-kicker (RED FLAG archetype)

**Signals — any one is enough**
- Showed up without a calendar invite or with someone unrelated to decision-making.
- "We're just exploring options" with no concrete pain or timeline.
- Asks "what would you charge for X" before describing X.
- Mentions "two or three other consultants" with no detail on why.
- Vague answers to "what would success look like?"

**What this archetype actually is**
- A buyer doing comp shopping for a project they may never start.
- A buyer using consultor calls as free education.
- An employee trying to look productive to their boss.

**Anchor strategy**
- Do not anchor. Qualify harder, ask for a written brief before call two, or politely walk.

**What NOT to pitch**
- Anything. This isn't a sale — it's a test of your time discipline.

## Appendix — Red flags across archetypes

These flags can appear inside any archetype. When present, they override the archetype's pricing/anchoring strategy and should be surfaced FIRST in any synthesis.

| Red flag | What it means | What to do |
|----------|---------------|------------|
| **No decision-maker on the call** | They sent a deputy; the real buyer is one layer behind. | Pricing anchor drops one tier; require decision-maker on call two or walk. |
| **"Te pago cuando funcione" / "I'll pay when it works"** | They don't see consultor work as labor. | Walk. Or quote 3× normal with 100% upfront. |
| **3+ prior consultors, all "their fault"** | Pattern. The buyer is the constant variable. | Walk, or aggressive premium for risk cover. |
| **1-week delivery demand** | Reality-detached urgency. | Scope down to one micro-deliverable in 1 week, premium price, written off-ramp. |
| **Vague pain ("¿qué podés hacer?")** | They haven't formed the problem yet. | Convert to paid discovery. Free → tire-kicker. |
| **Procurement-only contact** | No end-user pain on the call. | Request end-user on call two; if denied, walk. |
| **"Sobrino que sabe" comparison** | Anchored to amateur pricing. | Reframe to opportunity cost or walk. |
| **Fixed price for scope-unknown work** | They want certainty, you eat the variance. | Quote in phases with explicit scope per phase. |

When two or more red flags appear simultaneously, the right move is almost always to walk. The math on a bad fit is bad: 60 hours of work, scope creep, payment delays, and a damaged reputation cost more than the original deal price.
