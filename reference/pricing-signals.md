# Pricing Signals

How to anchor a price range from signals visible in a discovery call. The goal is not "what's this worth?" — it's "what range fits this buyer's signals?"

Same scope can land at $3K or $9K depending on the signals around it. Below: the base ranges, the multipliers, the LATAM-specific overlay, and the anti-patterns I avoid.

---

## Base ranges (USD, Phase 1 scope)

| Tier | Range | Fits |
|------|-------|------|
| **Small PYME** | $1–3K | 1–10 person business, single workflow, founder is the operator. |
| **Mid SaaS / clinic chain / mid agency** | $3–7K | 10–50 people, multi-workflow, dedicated operator role exists. |
| **Mature SMB / multi-location / cross-border** | $7–15K | 50+ people OR multi-channel/multi-location complexity OR cross-border ops. |

Phase 1 is always the *first* engagement. Subsequent phases are scoped and priced separately, usually 50–150% of Phase 1 depending on what was learned.

---

## Multipliers

Apply these to the base range, in order:

- **Urgency (real, not manufactured)** — concrete deadline within 8 weeks → **+30%**. "ASAP" without a deadline → no multiplier (it's noise).
- **No decision-maker on the call** — sent a deputy → **drop one tier or walk**. Without the buyer in the room you can't price competently and you'll waste cycles educating the wrong person.
- **Decision-maker on call + secondary stakeholder accessible** — no penalty, no premium. This is the default healthy state.
- **Prior bad-consultor experience** — buyer mentions an agency burn → **+10% trust premium**. They'll need more visible proof of work, more weekly check-ins, more reassurance — that's labor; price it in.
- **Referral from a successful prior client** — faster close cycle, no premium. Don't undercharge a referral; the referring client expects you to deliver the same quality.
- **Cold inbound from content (LinkedIn, blog, podcast)** — warmer than cold outbound, no premium added but expect a longer education cycle in the call.
- **Procurement-only contact, end-user not on call** — drop one tier or qualify harder. They want a vendor, not a partner.

## LATAM-specific overlay

- **USD vs ARS pricing** — for Argentinian clients, default to USD invoicing (wire or USDT). ARS pricing should include +30% over current official rate to account for inflation between sign and pay. If they push back, that's a signal about budget reality.
- **MX/CL conventions** — Mexico typically pays USD via wire without friction; Chile is similar. Both are easier than Argentina from an operations perspective; price the same as US clients.
- **When to invoice in dollars** — always for cross-border clients; for local LATAM clients, when project value is >$3K and they have any export revenue. Below that, ARS or local-currency stablecoin (USDT) is fine.
- **Family/friends-priced expectations** — LATAM SMB owners often have a "sobrino que sabe" anchor in their head ($200–500). When you sense this, your job is to reframe the comparison early ("he's a freelancer, I'm an operator with X years and Y track record"), not to defend your price reactively.

## Anti-patterns

Things I do not do, even when tempted:

- **"We'll see" pricing.** Quoting "$3–10K, depends on what we figure out" sounds flexible but reads as not-prepared. Always quote a Phase 1 number after the discovery call.
- **Fixed-fee for ambiguous scope.** If scope is unclear, sell a paid discovery (small fixed fee, $500–1500) that produces a written scope, then quote Phase 1 from that document. Never absorb scope variance.
- **"Te pago cuando funcione" / contingent pricing.** Decline the framing. (See `objection-bank.md` for the response pattern.)
- **Discounting without scope reduction.** If they push on price, the answer is always "I can scope down to fit X budget — what comes out?" — never "OK, I'll do it for less."
- **Pricing on cost-plus.** Time × hourly is a useful internal sanity check, never a quote justification. Buyers pay for outcomes and confidence, not your hours.

## A signal-based pricing example

Buyer is a 12-person SaaS founder with an 8-week board deadline ("ASAP"), prior agency burn, decision-maker on call, CTO accessible.

- Base: mid SaaS → $3–7K.
- Urgency +30%: $4K–9K.
- Prior bad agency +10% trust premium: $4.4K–$9.9K.
- Decision-maker present: no change.

→ **Quote $4–8K.** The range is honest about what scope variance can absorb, but the floor is firm.

The signals make the price defensible in 30 seconds, which is what the buyer needs to move forward.
